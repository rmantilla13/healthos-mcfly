healthOS

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 22, 2026

healthOS is a private health dashboard at health.mcfly.bot, run by Ricky Mantilla. It is not open to the public: accounts are created only with an invite code from an admin. Each person who joins gets their own dashboard, and members who share see each other on the Family board.

What data it uses

  • Your account: your email address, the name you enter, and your password, which is stored only as a one-way hash.
  • WHOOP, read through WHOOP's official API only after you sign in to WHOOP and approve access. healthOS reads recovery, sleep, physiological cycles and strain, workouts, basic profile details (first name) and body measurements. It never writes to WHOOP.
  • Apple Health, from the healthOS iPhone app or a Health export you import: daily totals and averages, steps, energy and heart rate by the hour, workouts, when you fell asleep and woke up, and the stages of each night (when you were in bed, awake, or in light, deep or REM sleep). The stages appear only on your own Sleep page; they're never on the Family board.
  • Your health profile, if you fill it in: the sex you choose for health calculations (female, male or prefer not to say).
  • Cycle tracking, which is off unless you turn it on: reproductive-health data, described in its own section below.
  • Nutrition, which is off unless you turn it on: daily totals of what you log in a food app, described in its own section below.
  • Lab results, if you add them: each result as printed, with the reference range the lab printed, typed in or read from a PDF or photo of a report you import. An imported report is sent to Anthropic to read the results and isn't kept; it counts as an Ask McFly question, and only the rows you check and save are stored. Only you (and McFly, when you ask it) see them: never the Family board, the admin screens, your summaries or the weekly digest. If you choose a sex in your health profile, it picks the typical adult range shown, labelled as such, for a result whose report printed no range.
  • An optional profile photo. It's cut out on your own device (in your browser, or on a Mac), and only the cut-out, a small avatar and the points where your vitals are shown are uploaded, never the original photo.
  • Ask McFly, the optional AI assistant: the questions you ask it. Your conversation is kept only in your browser tab, and healthOS stores just a daily count of your questions and how much processing they used.

How it is used

Your data is used to show you your own charts, trends and summaries and, while you share, your tile on the Family board. healthOS has no advertising, no analytics or tracking, and no profiling. It does not sell or rent data, or share it outside healthOS.

Ask McFly

When you ask McFly a question, healthOS sends your question, the conversation so far, and the parts of your health data McFly reads to answer it to Anthropic's API, which generates the reply. McFly can only read your own data, never other members', and it can't change anything. Anthropic handles this data under its commercial terms and privacy policy. If you don't use Ask McFly or import a lab report, nothing is sent to Anthropic. McFly can read your cycle tracking data only while you have cycle tracking turned on, and your nutrition only while nutrition is on, and only as much of it as you chose to see.

Cycle tracking

Cycle tracking is reproductive-health data, so it has extra protections. It stays off until you turn on Track my cycle in Settings. Anyone can turn it on: it doesn't depend on the sex you choose.

  • What it covers: menstrual flow and the first day of each period, spotting, ovulation test results, cervical mucus, basal body temperature, symptoms, mornings you mark as having an unreliable temperature (for example after alcohol or while sick), and sexual activity, including whether protection was used. It comes from Apple Health and from what you log on the Cycle page.
  • While it's off: if your Apple Health export, or the iPhone app once you allow it to read cycle data, includes these records, healthOS still stores them, hidden from every page and from Ask McFly, so your history is there if you turn tracking on. Turning tracking off hides the data again. It doesn't delete it.
  • Estimates: your next period, ovulation and fertile window are calculated from your own logged cycles and, while tracking is on, the nightly temperature your Apple Watch or WHOOP already records. Until you have logged two cycles, they also lean on typical cycle lengths from published research. The Cycle page also compares your resting heart rate, HRV and temperature before and after ovulation. All of this is calculated on healthOS's own server, and none of it is fed back into your summaries or insights. They are estimates. They are not medical advice, and they are not reliable as contraception.
  • Who sees it: only you, unless you share your phase on the Family board. It is kept apart from your other health data. Apart from a phase you share, it never appears in your summaries or insights, in the admin screens, in the server's logs, or in web addresses.
  • Ask McFly: while tracking is on, McFly can read this data, and the parts it reads to answer a question are sent to Anthropic as described above.
  • Deleting it: Delete all cycle data, in Settings or on the Cycle page, removes every cycle entry at once, whether tracking is on or off. Entries from Apple Health come back with your next import or iPhone sync unless you also delete them in the Health app or turn off healthOS's access to them in the iPhone's Health settings.

Nutrition

What you eat and drink is sensitive, so nutrition has the same kind of protections as cycle tracking. It stays off until you turn on Nutrition in Settings or on the Nutrition page, and only you can turn it on.

  • What it covers: daily totals of what you log in a food app, such as MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It!, MacroFactor or Yazio, that writes to Apple Health: calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sugar, saturated fat, sodium and alcoholic drinks, with the name of the app that logged them. It comes from the healthOS iPhone app or your Apple Health export. healthOS doesn't read single foods or meal photos.
  • What you see: when you turn it on, you choose everything, everything except calories, or no amounts at all. Whatever you leave out is removed on the server, so it never reaches the page or Ask McFly. The Nutrition page also shows the water and caffeine you already log, and patterns between them and your sleep and heart rate variability, worded as things that tend to go together, not causes.
  • Targets: healthOS never suggests one. You can set your own for calories, protein and water in Settings; each shows only as a plain line on your charts, never as a score.
  • While it's off: healthOS doesn't read or keep your food totals. Turning it off hides what's already stored. It doesn't delete it.
  • Who sees it: only you. It is kept apart from your other health data. It never appears on the Family board, in your summaries, insights or weekly digest, in the admin screens, in the server's logs, or in web addresses.
  • Ask McFly: while nutrition is on, McFly can read it, as much as you chose to see, and the parts it reads to answer a question are sent to Anthropic as described above.
  • Deleting it: Delete all nutrition data in Settings removes every food total at once, whether nutrition is on or off. Removing your Apple Health data removes it too. With nutrition on, totals still in Apple Health come back with your next import or iPhone sync.

Family board

Members see each other on the Family board. Sharing is on unless you turn off Share with family in Settings, and it's mutual: turn it off and you neither see anyone nor are seen.

  • Your tile shows: your name and avatar, which devices you use, your local date and when your newest data is from, last night's sleep (WHOOP's total includes naps), WHOOP recovery or else heart rate variability (from WHOOP or Apple Watch), your resting heart rate, today's steps and the hour they were counted to, your training load (WHOOP strain, or active energy), the last 30 days of each of these, your usual for each, and a status that compares you only with your own last 30 days. Tapping your tile shows those 30 days as charts.
  • Never on it: your email, step goal, workouts, weight, temperature, your sleep stages, anything about food, drink or nutrition, anything older than 30 days, or anything you ask McFly.
  • Cycle phase: only if you also turn on Show my cycle phase to family. It shows one word (period, follicular or luteal), never dates or logs, and it disappears as your next period approaches. Turning it on tells members that you track your cycle, and someone who looks day after day can tell roughly when your period starts. Turning off cycle tracking or sharing turns it off too.
  • Changes reach pages that are already open within about 5 minutes. The status is a nudge to check in, not medical advice.

Who can see it

  • Your full health data is visible only to you. Other members see only your Family board tile and its 30 days, and only while you both share.
  • Admins manage accounts. They can see your email, name, role, when you last signed in, and the health of your syncs: how many days of data you have and its date range, when Apple Health and WHOOP last synced, whether a sync failed, and how many questions you asked McFly. The admin screens don't show your health values or your questions. In the admin screens, admins never see your cycle tracking data, your nutrition, your health profile, or whether you have cycle tracking or nutrition turned on. An admin who shares sees the Family board like any other member, including a phase you share.
  • Admins can disable or delete your account and create a password-reset link for you. They never see or set your password.
  • The operator runs the database and so has technical access to it, but does not look at members' health data.

Where it is stored

  • The app is hosted on Vercel. Data is stored in a Postgres database run by Neon in the United States (US East).
  • All traffic is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).
  • Reading any data requires signing in. The only cookie is a sign-in session cookie, which is strictly necessary.
  • WHOOP access tokens and iPhone app tokens are stored server-side and are never exposed to other people.

Service providers

These providers handle data only to run the service: WHOOP (a data source), Vercel (hosting), Neon (database) and Anthropic (answers for Ask McFly, and reading lab reports you import). Their own privacy policies apply to their services.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Your data is kept until you remove it or your account is deleted.
  • You can remove your Apple Health data from the Integrations page at any time.
  • You can delete all your cycle tracking data from Settings or the Cycle page at any time.
  • You can delete all your nutrition data from Settings at any time.
  • Disconnecting WHOOP in healthOS revokes the app's access with WHOOP and deletes the stored tokens. You can also revoke access from WHOOP's own account settings.
  • To delete your account, ask the admin. Deleting an account removes everything stored for it: health data, settings, tokens and photo.
