healthOS

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 24, 2026

healthOS is a private health dashboard at health.mcfly.bot, run by Ricky Mantilla. It is not open to the public: accounts are created only with an invite code from an admin. Each person who joins gets their own dashboard, and members who share see each other on the Family board.

What data it uses

  • Your account: your email address, the name you enter, and your password, which is stored only as a one-way hash.
  • WHOOP, read through WHOOP's official API only after you sign in to WHOOP and approve access. healthOS reads recovery, sleep, physiological cycles and strain, workouts, basic profile details (first name) and body measurements. It never writes to WHOOP.
  • Apple Health, from the healthOS iPhone app or a Health export you import: daily totals and averages, steps, energy and heart rate by the hour, workouts, when you fell asleep and woke up, and the stages of each night (when you were in bed, awake, or in light, deep or REM sleep). The stages appear only on your own Sleep page; they're never on the Family board.
  • Fitbit and Google Health exports, if you import one on the Integrations page: healthOS reads the export (from Google Takeout) in your browser, on your device, and the file itself is never uploaded. Only per-day readings and each night's sleep stretches are sent to your account, marked as Fitbit: heart rate, resting heart rate, HRV, blood oxygen, breathing rate, steps, sleep times and stages, weight and body fat. The rest of the export (your profile, calories, exercise, settings) is never opened, and nothing is sent to Google or Fitbit.
  • About you, from the first-run setup ("Make this space yours") and your Profile: how Home and Body show you (your portrait, a digital human or just the statistics), how you describe yourself (never used for health calculations), and your age, stored as the age you gave and the day you gave it, so no birth date is kept.
  • Your health profile, if you fill it in: the sex you choose for health calculations (female, male or prefer not to say).
  • Cycle tracking, which is off unless you turn it on: reproductive-health data, described in its own section below.
  • Nutrition, which is off unless you turn it on: what you log in healthOS or in a food app, described in its own section below.
  • Body measurements you add (weight, body fat, lean body mass): each entry with its date, an optional time, where it came from (scale, scan or records) and an optional note. Each day's latest entry is also saved alongside your device readings, so your charts, the detail sheets and Ask McFly use it, and it takes the place of a device's reading for that day. Clearing Apple Health or disconnecting WHOOP leaves them in place.
  • Blood records, if you add them: each result as printed, with the reference range the lab printed, typed in or read from a report. A report you upload (a PDF or a photo, up to 4 MB) is kept in your account under Lab documents, where only you can open it. Its results are read only when you choose Read results: the report is then sent to Anthropic, on your own Claude key if you connected one, otherwise as one of your Ask McFly questions, and only the rows you check and save are stored as results. Only you (and McFly, when you ask it) see your results: never the Family board, the admin screens, your summaries or the weekly digest. If you choose a sex in your health profile, it picks the typical adult range shown, labelled as such, for a result whose report printed no range.
  • Your journal, if you write in it: each check-in's words, an optional mood, and its date and time. Your journal is private: it isn't shared on the Family board, in peer comparisons or with Ask McFly, and the admin screens never show it. Your own Home shows today's latest check-in (its mood and first line). A check-in you haven't saved yet is kept only in that browser tab.
  • An optional profile photo. It's cut out on your own device (in your browser, or on a Mac), and only the cut-out, a small avatar and the points where your vitals are shown are uploaded, never the original photo. Portrait reference photos are different: see Portraits below.
  • Ask McFly, the optional AI assistant: the questions you ask it. Your conversation is kept only in your browser tab, and healthOS stores just a daily count of your questions and how much processing they used.
  • Your own AI keys, if you connect one: described in its own section below.

How it is used

Your data is used to show you your own charts, trends and summaries and, while you share, your tile on the Family board. healthOS has no advertising, no analytics or tracking, and no profiling. It does not sell or rent data, or share it outside healthOS, apart from the providers named below for the features you use. If you turn on peer comparisons, your 30-day averages also count toward anonymous group figures, described below.

Ask McFly

When you ask McFly a question, healthOS sends your question, the conversation so far, and the parts of your health data McFly reads to answer it to the AI provider that answers for you: Anthropic's API on healthOS's shared key, unless you connected your own key and chose its provider (see Your own AI keys). McFly can only read your own data, never other members', and it can't change anything. Each provider handles this data under its own terms and privacy policy. If you don't use Ask McFly, estimate a meal or ask healthOS to read a lab report, nothing is sent to an AI provider. McFly can read your cycle tracking data only while you have cycle tracking turned on, and your nutrition only while nutrition is on, and only as much of it as you chose to see. It never reads your journal.

Your own AI keys

  • Storing a key: you can connect your own OpenAI, Anthropic (Claude) or Google (Gemini) API key in your Profile. healthOS checks it with that provider once, then stores it encrypted (AES-256-GCM, bound to your account). It is never shown again (you see its last 4 characters), never logged, and never included in your data export. Removing the key, or deleting your account, deletes it.
  • Who receives what: without a key of your own, Ask McFly and meal estimates use healthOS's shared Claude key (Anthropic), as before. With your own key, the provider you chose for the chatbot receives your Ask McFly questions and the data McFly reads to answer them. Meal photos and descriptions go to your own OpenAI key if you connected one, otherwise your own Claude key, otherwise the shared Claude key. OpenAI and Google receive nothing unless you connect them (or, for voice notes and portraits, unless the admin set a server OpenAI key). Ask McFly and estimate requests to OpenAI are sent with its response storage turned off. On Gemini, use a paid-tier key: under Google's terms, free-tier content may be used to improve its products and read by human reviewers.
  • Voice notes: a voice note is recorded in your browser and sent once to OpenAI to be turned into words, using your own OpenAI key, or the server's if the admin set one. healthOS never stores or logs the recording. The words fill the message box for you to check, and nothing is estimated until you send it. On the server's key, each person can send up to 40 voice notes a day (counted by UTC date), and one OpenAI couldn't turn into words isn't counted. Voice notes on your own OpenAI key aren't limited, and a voice note never counts as an Ask McFly question.

Cycle tracking

Cycle tracking is reproductive-health data, so it has extra protections. It stays off until you turn on Track my cycle in your Profile. Anyone can turn it on: it doesn't depend on the sex you choose.

  • What it covers: menstrual flow and the first day of each period, spotting, ovulation test results, cervical mucus, basal body temperature, symptoms, mornings you mark as having an unreliable temperature (for example after alcohol or while sick), and sexual activity, including whether protection was used. It comes from Apple Health and from what you log on the Cycle page.
  • While it's off: if your Apple Health export, or the iPhone app once you allow it to read cycle data, includes these records, healthOS still stores them, hidden from every page and from Ask McFly, so your history is there if you turn tracking on. Turning tracking off hides the data again. It doesn't delete it.
  • Estimates: your next period, ovulation and fertile window are calculated from your own logged cycles and, while tracking is on, the nightly temperature your Apple Watch or WHOOP already records. Until you have logged two cycles, they also lean on typical cycle lengths from published research. The Cycle page also compares your resting heart rate, HRV and temperature before and after ovulation. All of this is calculated on healthOS's own server, and none of it is fed back into your summaries or insights. They are estimates. They are not medical advice, and they are not reliable as contraception.
  • Who sees it: only you, unless you share your phase on the Family board. It is kept apart from your other health data. Apart from a phase you share, it never appears in your summaries or insights, in the admin screens, in the server's logs, or in web addresses.
  • Ask McFly: while tracking is on, McFly can read this data, and the parts it reads to answer a question are sent to the AI provider that answers for you, as described above.
  • Deleting it: Delete all cycle data, in your Profile or on the Cycle page, removes every cycle entry at once, whether tracking is on or off. Entries from Apple Health come back with your next import or iPhone sync unless you also delete them in the Health app or turn off healthOS's access to them in the iPhone's Health settings.

Nutrition

What you eat and drink is sensitive, so nutrition has the same kind of protections as cycle tracking. It stays off until you turn on Nutrition in your Profile or on the Nutrition page, and only you can turn it on.

  • What it covers: daily totals of what you log in a food app, such as MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It!, MacroFactor or Yazio, that writes to Apple Health: calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sugar, saturated fat, sodium and alcoholic drinks, with the name of the app that logged them. It comes from the healthOS iPhone app or your Apple Health export. healthOS doesn't read those apps' single foods or meal photos.
  • Logging in healthOS: you can also log food and drink here, on the web or the iPhone. Each entry keeps its name, portion, time and amounts. A day's entries count as one more app: when another app also logged that day, the page shows one app's totals, as it does for any two apps, so nothing is counted twice.
    • Searching and barcodes: what you type in the search box, or the barcode you scan, is sent from the healthOS server to USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts, without anything that identifies you. healthOS doesn't keep what you searched for. It keeps the foods they return, shared by everyone, so the next lookup needs no network.
    • Estimates from a photo, a description or a voice note: tell McFly what you ate and it answers with an estimate to check. The photo (shrunk on your device first, which also removes its location) and the words go to one provider for that one estimate: your own OpenAI key if you connected one, otherwise your own Claude key, otherwise healthOS's shared Claude key (Anthropic). healthOS never stores or logs them. On the shared key it counts as one McFly question toward the daily limit. The estimate is a rough draft, often a third off on calories, so nothing is saved until you check it and save it. An estimate you don't save or discard is kept for up to a day, counted nowhere, and then deleted. The conversation itself, including your words and small copies of your photos, is kept only in your browser tab and goes when you close it. Voice notes are described under Your own AI keys.
    • Apple Health: the iPhone app can save what you log there to Apple Health, if you allow it, with only the amounts you chose to see (no calories if you hide them, and nothing at all if you see no amounts). healthOS then skips those entries when it reads Apple Health back, so they aren't counted twice. Once in Apple Health, they follow your Health settings, and other apps you allow can read them.
  • What you see: when you turn it on, you choose everything, everything except calories, or no calorie or nutrient amounts at all (what you logged and when still shows, with its portion). Whatever you leave out is removed on the server, so it never reaches the page, Ask McFly or your data export. The Nutrition page also shows the water and caffeine you already log, and patterns between them and your sleep and heart rate variability, worded as things that tend to go together, not causes.
  • Targets: healthOS never suggests one. You can set your own for calories, protein and water in your Profile; each shows only as a plain line on your charts, never as a score.
  • While it's off: healthOS doesn't read or keep your food totals, and you can't log, search or estimate food. Turning it off hides what's already stored. It doesn't delete it.
  • Who sees it: only you. It is kept apart from your other health data. It never appears on the Family board, in your summaries, insights or weekly digest, in the admin screens, in the server's logs, or in web addresses.
  • Ask McFly: while nutrition is on, McFly can read it, as much as you chose to see, and the parts it reads to answer a question are sent to the AI provider that answers for you, as described above.
  • Deleting it: Delete all nutrition data in your Profile removes every food total and every entry you logged in healthOS at once, whether nutrition is on or off. You can also delete a single entry. Removing your Apple Health data removes the totals read from it, but not what you logged in healthOS. With nutrition on, totals still in Apple Health come back with your next import or iPhone sync. Entries the iPhone saved to Apple Health stay there until you delete them in the Health app.

Family board

Members see each other on the Family board. Sharing is on unless you turn off Share with family in your Profile, and it's mutual: turn it off and you neither see anyone nor are seen.

  • Your tile shows: your name and avatar, which devices you use, your local date and when your newest data is from, last night's sleep (WHOOP's total includes naps), WHOOP recovery or else heart rate variability (from WHOOP or Apple Watch), your resting heart rate, today's steps and the hour they were counted to, your training load (WHOOP strain, or active energy), the last 30 days of each of these, your usual for each, and a status that compares you only with your own last 30 days. Tapping your tile shows those 30 days as charts.
  • Never on it: your email, step goal, workouts, weight and the body measurements you add, temperature, your sleep stages, anything about food, drink or nutrition, your journal, your blood records, your age or how you describe yourself, anything older than 30 days, or anything you ask McFly.
  • Cycle phase: only if you also turn on Show my cycle phase to family. It shows one word (period, follicular or luteal), never dates or logs, and it disappears as your next period approaches. Turning it on tells members that you track your cycle, and someone who looks day after day can tell roughly when your period starts. Turning off cycle tracking or sharing turns it off too.
  • Changes reach pages that are already open within about 5 minutes. The status is a nudge to check in, not medical advice.

Peer comparisons

Peer comparisons are off unless you turn them on, and only from age 18. While they're on, your 30-day averages count toward anonymous figures for people in your age band (18–29, 30–39, … 70+) who also take part, for the same measurement from the same source (for example resting heart rate from WHOOP). It's mutual: only people taking part see results. Results appear only when at least 20 other people each have 7 recorded days, and show only the group's rounded median and quartiles with a size range (20+, 50+, 100+), and which quarter of the group your own average falls in: never names, photos, accounts, counts or anyone's own readings, and nobody can see who takes part. Your quarter describes where you sit in this group; a higher one doesn't necessarily mean better health. Turning it off takes effect immediately. Your journal, nutrition, cycle and blood records are never used.

Portraits

If you choose "Your own portrait", you can upload reference photos of yourself. Your browser shrinks each one and removes its metadata (location, time, camera) before upload. They are stored in your account and only you can open them; admins cannot. Unlike the profile photo above, a reference photo does leave your device. It is sent to OpenAI only when you choose Make my portrait, and only for that request, on your own OpenAI key if you connected one, otherwise on this server's OpenAI key. Portraits made from it, including attempts that failed, are stored in your account until you delete them. None of them appears on your dashboard until you choose Use this portrait: it's then cut out on your device, like any profile photo, and becomes the figure and avatar on Home, Body, the Family board and the iPhone app.

Who can see it

  • Your full health data is visible only to you. Other members see only your Family board tile and its 30 days, and only while you both share.
  • Admins manage accounts. They can see your email, name, role, when you last signed in, and the health of your syncs: how many days of data you have and its date range, when Apple Health and WHOOP last synced, whether a sync failed, and how many questions you asked McFly. The admin screens don't show your health values or your questions. In the admin screens, admins never see your cycle tracking data, your nutrition, your health profile, your age or how you describe yourself, your journal, your blood records or lab documents, your body measurements, your portraits, your AI keys, whether you take part in peer comparisons, or whether you have cycle tracking or nutrition turned on. An admin who shares sees the Family board like any other member, including a phase you share.
  • Admins can disable or delete your account and create a password-reset link for you. They never see or set your password.
  • The operator runs the database and so has technical access to it, but does not look at members' health data.

Where it is stored

  • The app is hosted on Vercel. Data is stored in a Postgres database run by Neon in the United States (US East).
  • All traffic is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).
  • Reading any data requires signing in. The only cookie is a sign-in session cookie, which is strictly necessary.
  • WHOOP access tokens and iPhone app tokens are stored server-side and are never exposed to other people.
  • Your own AI keys are stored encrypted, with a key the server holds separately from the database.
  • Lab reports and portrait photos you upload are stored in the same database and served only to you; reports are served in a sandbox, so a file can't run anything on the site.
  • The app's fonts are served by healthOS itself, so loading a page contacts no one else.

Service providers

These providers handle data only to run the service: WHOOP (a data source), Vercel (hosting), Neon (database) and Anthropic (answers for Ask McFly and food estimates on the shared key, and reading a lab report when you ask it to). OpenAI and Google (Gemini) receive data only for people who connect their own key for them; OpenAI also transcribes voice notes and makes portraits, on your key or a server key the admin set. With nutrition on, food searches and barcodes go to USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts, without anything that identifies you. Their own privacy policies apply to their services.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Your data is kept until you remove it or your account is deleted.
  • You can remove your Apple Health data, or your Fitbit data, from the Integrations page at any time.
  • You can delete all your cycle tracking data from your Profile or the Cycle page at any time.
  • You can delete all your nutrition data, including the food you logged in healthOS, from your Profile at any time.
  • You can delete any body measurement you added (on Body), any lab report (Blood records → Lab documents), one journal check-in or all of them (Journal), any portrait or reference photo (Profile → Your portrait photos), and any AI key you connected (Profile).
  • You can download all your data as a JSON file from your Profile (Export your data): your readings, sleep, workouts, food log, cycle entries, blood records, journal, body measurements, settings, and a list of your files (name, type, size and dates; not the files themselves). Passwords, keys and connection tokens are never included, and neither are the nutrition amounts your Nutrition setting hides.
  • Disconnecting WHOOP in healthOS revokes the app's access with WHOOP and deletes the stored tokens. You can also revoke access from WHOOP's own account settings.
  • To delete your account, ask the admin. Deleting an account removes everything stored for it: health data, journal, blood records and lab documents, body measurements, portraits, settings, keys, tokens and photo.
